How the ACQ platform works

One laptop, one offline router, a room full of phones. The design choices, and the reasoning behind them:

Local-first

The server runs on the host's laptop and phones join over a Wi-Fi network with no internet. Venue Wi-Fi can't kill the quiz, latency stays around 5 to 20 ms, and teams can't google the answers (mobile data off, volunteers watching).

Zero dependencies

Python's standard library only: http.server, sqlite3, vanilla JS and a hand-written QR encoder. No pip install, no Node, no build step, so nothing can break at 7:55 pm.

Server-Sent Events, not WebSockets

Updates only flow one way, which is exactly what SSE does, and browsers reconnect by themselves. Answers are plain POSTs, so a dropped stream can't lose one. A polling fallback catches stalled connections.

Answers survive anything

Every action is committed to SQLite before the phone is told it succeeded. Saves are idempotent and ordered, phones keep a local copy first, and killing the server mid-question just resumes it.

The client is hostile

The rules live on the server: a state machine, one answer per team enforced by the database, and payloads built per role so a phone never receives another team's answer. The UI hiding a button is a courtesy, not security.

Humans mark, software helps

Only normalised exact matches are scored automatically. Similar answers are suggested, never scored (1947 and 1948 are one edit apart), and identical answers are grouped, so 40 wrong teams are one decision.

Total infrastructure bill: one laptop, one router, zero cloud.

Travel forecasting

Forecasting 43,890 corridors

Problem
Average Length of Stay for every travel corridor. Far too many to model by hand.
Approach
Out-of-sample-validated gravity models, automatic model selection per corridor (MAPE, pseudo-R², sign consistency, mean reversion), topline scaling with soft bounds that preserves rank and trend.
Result
One reproducible pipeline, weak corridors flagged for an analyst instead of shipping quietly, and methodology docs co-authored with the team.
